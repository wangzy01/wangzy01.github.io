Under review

Decision-Ready Human Motion Forecasting: Calibrated, Robot-Responsive Futures Within One Perception Cycle

A robot acts on how many of the forecast futures come within a warning distance, and when, not on the one future closest to what happened.

DAVE-Motion (Delivery, Accuracy, and Value Evaluation of Human Motion Forecasts for Robots in Shared Spaces) scores the delivery, accuracy, and decision value of forecasts at every precaution cost. DeciMo, a Decision-ready Motion forecaster, trains the set the robot reads, as the robot reads it, and delivers 50 futures within one frame.

01Section

Accuracy is not decision value

Rankings by best-of-K error agree only moderately with the decision value of DAVE-Motion and can reverse.

In the HARPER window at the top of the page, the closest futures of DeciMo and CoMusion are almost equally accurate, yet a robot that acts on the whole set slows down early with DeciMo and stops late with CoMusion. Over the whole HARPER test set, best-of-K ADE, which scores only the forecast future closest to the recorded one, ranks eleven forecasters (each with and without the robot history) in an order that agrees only moderately with the decision value that a robot draws from the whole set: Kendall’s τ between the two rankings is 0.45 with the robot history and 0.56 without it. The order can reverse even between two generators of 50 futures: with the human history alone, SPARD has a 6% lower ADE than SkeletonDiffusion, yet a PBS of 0.703 against 0.795.

02Section

What a forecast is worth to a robot

The value of acting on the forecast, for every cost of precaution.

Each proper event score of DAVE-Motion averages the regret of robots that act on the forecast. For a robot whose precaution costs a fraction α of the loss it avoids, the relative value V(α) is 1 for knowing the future and 0 for knowing only the base rate. The precaution-band skill (PBS) summarizes it over cost ratios from 0.02 to 0.2, where precaution is cheap, as the share of the base-rate regret that the forecast removes for these robots.

04Section

Forecasts that respond to the robot and to the person

The futures change with what the robot does and with subtle cues of the person.

4.1The robot history matters

People react to the robot, so the futures must change with its motion. On HARPER, the robot history improves the forecasts of DeciMo the most: it lowers the best-of-K ADE of DeciMo by 11% and the share of entries it misses by 66%, against at most 3% and 33% for the other forecasters.

5.2The test windows

Four datasets pair people with a legged robot (HARPER), a mobile base (UF-Retail-HRI), an arm that the person guides by hand (COMFI), and an industrial arm (CHICO). Their test windows differ in how close the person starts, whether the person is approaching, and how fast the robot moves, and with them in how often a person comes within 0.2 m in the next second.

06Section

Method

DeciMo follows one principle: train the set the robot reads, as the robot reads it.

6.1DeciMo

  1. One-pass generation. DeciMo generates each future from its own noise in one network pass: a draft network trained with an average-velocity objective and with trajectory displacement consistency, which anchors its jump in direction and length to the futures recorded for the condition, followed by a refiner that repairs the draft against the history. The set is thus cheap to generate at deployment and inside the training loop.
  2. Condition-consistent multi-future learning. Each training history learns several futures. Borrowing them by pose alone would divide the robot’s effect on every event probability of the training targets by the number of futures per history, an effect called condition dilution. DeciMo therefore borrows futures only from training windows recorded under a compatible condition, including where the robot is and how it moves, so that the set responds to the robot.
  3. Deployed-set training. Training generates whole sets exactly as at inference and fits them with proper scores on body motion and on entries and exits. On human–robot data, a set head with at most 0.87M parameters refines the K futures jointly.

6.2Calibration and the set head

On HARPER, the entry probabilities of DeciMo follow the observed frequencies. The set head, which refines the 50 futures jointly, raises the precaution-band skill on 76 of the 90 questions. At the reference question, it lifts the precaution-band skill from 0.843 to 0.867.

Abstract

Safety standards make robots that share space with people plan for the worst case.

Read the full abstract

On HARPER, this calls for a precaution in 94% of the windows with a person outside 0.2 m, but the person comes that close within 1 s in only 15% of the flagged windows. Forecasts could spare such precautions, but forecasters are designed and evaluated for their best sample, whereas a robot reads from the whole set the probability and timing of a person entering or leaving a warning distance. We call a forecaster decision-ready if, within one perception cycle, it delivers a set of futures whose entry and exit distributions are calibrated, sharp, and responsive to the robot. Our protocol, DAVE-Motion (Delivery, Accuracy, and Value Evaluation of Human Motion Forecasts for Robots in Shared Spaces), scores delivery, accuracy, and decision value at every precaution cost. DeciMo, a Decision-ready Motion forecaster, meets these requirements by design, training the set the robot reads as the robot reads it: it generates each future in one pass, borrows training futures only from compatible robot states, and trains the delivered set with proper scores on entries and exits, so its probabilities are calibrated. Across four robot types, it delivers 50 futures within one frame, with the lowest best-of-K errors and the highest decision value of eleven forecasters. On HARPER, it misses 2.0% of entries at a false alarm rate of at most 10% (others: at least 5.3%), and the robot history lowers its ADE by 11% (others: at most 3%).
